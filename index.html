How it works

Colourbond fences can’t rake with the ground. Posts stay plumb (perfectly vertical) and panels stay level. Any fall is taken as equal vertical steps at each bay.

Step height = total fall ÷ (posts − 1)

Number of steps = number of bays = posts − 1. Each step is the same measurement so the fence looks even down the run.

How it works

Split the run into the fewest equal bays that stay within your max centres.

Bays = ceil(length ÷ max bay)
Centres = length ÷ bays
Posts = bays + 1

That keeps every bay the same width and never over your maximum.

With Colourbond posts on timber, centres are still timber-to-timber. Clear bay (rail/panel piece length) ≈ centres − timber width.

How it works

Lap and cap: a back row of palings with gaps, then a front row where each paling covers a gap and laps onto the back palings either side.

Back gap = paling width − 2 × lap (150 − 50 = 100 mm)
Each paling adds = width − lap (125 mm), so a back + front pair covers 250 mm
Palings per metre = 1000 ÷ (width − lap) (8 for 150 / 25)
Total palings = 1 + ceil((run − width) ÷ (width − lap))

Palings are counted along the full run, starting with a back paling at the start of the run. They run straight past the posts (the palings are fixed to the rails, not between posts), so there’s no waste or doubling up at each post. Per bay is an average for reference.

Posts: bays = ceil(length ÷ max), centres = length ÷ bays, posts = bays + 1 (same as Post spacing).

Rail pieces = rails per bay × bays (each ≈ centres, joined on posts)
Total rail length = run length × rails per bay
Nails = palings × rails × nails per rail

Buy a few spare palings for splits and offcuts. Concrete is not included.
